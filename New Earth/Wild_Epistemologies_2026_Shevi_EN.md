# Introduction

Awww.

Leaning between a world in flames and famous couples who are just a bit too perfect, curated algorithms and galloping inflation, there is no doubt that we find ourselves in the thick of hypernormalization.

The divergence between the everyday reality of most people and the Instagrammed fiction is no accident — it is designed to create the feeling that one is the maladjusted one. It is the optical illusion that enables the privatization of collapse, the reversal of the burden of proof onto the anonymous entities driving our extinction.

It is remarkable how much of a creative vacuum exists in Hollywood's audiovisual output, where everything is remakes, reboots, and perennial franchises — as though fiction has migrated off-screen. Almost as if films were mere pretexts for weaving narratives in 3D, that is to say, the paratext has become the main course, fed by parasocial bonds — romances leap from the screen into real life, and the fictional discourse overflows into promotional interviews. Stars hire 360-degree fictional public relations services, specifically designed to create a parasol, a projective barrier between themselves and their fans. In this way, they feed the insatiable algorithm, do lucrative business, promote their brand, while simultaneously protecting their privacy.

This strategy makes perfect sense, because no one goes to the cinema anymore, but everyone comments on social media.

What follows is a dismantling — through abandonment, neglect, or perhaps planning — of the source of creation and sustenance of symbolic, collective, expansive, and cohesive public infrastructures. Cinema and television were, for much of the twentieth century and the early twenty-first, the third preferred place, the comfort zone where shared values, aesthetics, debates, progress, and limits were forged and validated. The screen was the continuation of the nation by other means.

Thus, stripped of the horizontal plane, the panoramic gaze, the bodily ritual, and the magical cycle, we are left with only vertical time — fragmented and privatized. Windows of permitted voyeurism toward a simulated, performative, and monetized intimacy. Literature is living through a similar moment, divided between large productions by popular authors (those with a built-in audience, social media followers) and the calcified circuits of academic dinosaurs trafficking prestige tickets among themselves. Series and podcasts, once insurgent spaces of creativity without budget, have also been devoured by the monetizing monster.

This broken mirror is coherent with an era of ecocide, genocide, omnicicide. Everyone is striving to return to the pre-pandemic routine, to make the new normal simply the normal, full stop.

We keep talking about marketing, about the children's school, about GDP; we celebrate grandmother's birthday, watch the match, write the quarterly report, go on holiday. But the bedrock of our reality disintegrates minute by minute beneath our feet — we are building our lives in mid-air.

It is as though the capitalist realism Mark Fisher described were doubling down on itself, a performativity identical to its intention: the drought of symbols, myths, narratives, spaces of containment, and relational fabric exposes us to ontological exposure and existential risk.

The material structure of shared spaces — the cinema, the park, the cultural center — degrades at the same speed as the symbolic barrier, revealing the desert of the Real.

The creeping normality — the gradual normalization of social (anomic), environmental, and technological disintegration — lays bare before our eyes the undeniable threat of the terminal risk to the species. This is fine.

A natural reaction is the exacerbation of rage, aggression, explosiveness, and the release of compulsions. Levels of civility and sanity — in other words — decline alarmingly once the rules loosen or blur. The accelerated descent into darkness and madness seems a canonical requirement of any apocalyptic scenario.

Another natural reaction is denial — a mechanism of self-preservation that is far from naive, but rather sophisticated, of our species. And it is that, in the midst of civilizational collapse, no one is speaking to people: not celebrities, not politicians, not businesspeople, not scientists, not artists, not religions.

That is to say, those spheres responsible for making sense of things, for enabling and sustaining symbolic highways for the healthy, safe, and productive channeling of our biospiritural effort, of our time on earth, are out of reach, mute, absent, or even actively participating in the stripping of the burning building.

It is perhaps this explicit, obscene performance in the economic and cultural dismantling that is the most wounding slap. Our faith in leaderships and institutions is returned to us as a vile gesture; it smiles, perverse, from the screens of the very devices that incite us to civic action, knowing in advance that it will prove useless and powerless. Our psyche, then, galvanizes and takes the most benevolent, least conflictive pills for its prior constitution, in order to guarantee its continuity.

Don't Look Up.

Of course, another option exists.

We can face this crisis with open eyes, head-on, with a strategy. We have a unique opportunity of access to the Real, now that the symbolic interface is melting, and we can approach it without the usual conditionings, through decentralized, ad hoc, or ancestral tools.

That is to say: wild epistemologies.

This is an excellent moment to remember that what we call reality is a collective, consensual construction, in which the imaginary and matter meet in a continuous tautological loop. The stable, continuous correspondence of these two poles is what gives us reassurance.

This feeling of security, however, can become addictive and block us from recognizing that we are operating under a model, a map — three-dimensional and realistic, that is, a simulation. That is why we call it the matrix. But in moments of terminal risk, of ontological disintegration, the fetishization of the status quo — operated through material or scientistic reductionism — constitutes a risk, an analytical defect that can be lethal.

The deviance police is very active in times of calculated dismantling, and its primary task consists in reinforcing hegemonic epistemology to effectively block alternative hermeneutic routes, consolidate this timeline, and project an effect of singular reality.

This is not a conspiracy; it is a tacit agenda that includes well-intentioned actors — and for that very reason it proves all the more effective and lethal.

Each person is thus left to their own devices, their abilities, and their resources. Of course, building community is a survival imperative, but it is not enough: it is essential to arm oneself with two instruments of salvation.

One is the wild epistemology — sophisticated, coherent, robust, flexible, rich, and elegant — that allows us to read scenarios correctly and navigate them with safety and ease.

The other, dear reader, is faith.


# Esoterophobia and Simulacrum

As mentioned in the introduction, reductionism is not so much a neutral intellectual position (which does not exist) as it is an extreme defensive strategy against the horror vacui of the uncharted.

We regard reductionism as a form of extreme neurosis in the face of the abyss of the Real, whose emergence is immediately pathologized and disdained.

This phobia of what exceeds the known and sanctioned constitutes a compulsive mechanism, a self-defense against what cannot be symbolized through standard signifiers or parameters, what escapes the register of conventional language and socially validated representation.

Scientistic, materialist reductionism is nothing other than a manifestation of intolerance to ambiguity and a total refraction of mystery. It is not merely the negation of metaphysics: it is a constant, exhausting effort to collapse uncertainty at every moment, a demonstration of extreme, binary, authoritarian, and final control. It is a psyche that cannot tolerate certain types of encounter with the indeterminate, the liminal, the transitional, the glitch, the paradox, the multiplicity, the tangent, the unexpected, the ambivalent, the potential state — in short, any quantum glimpse of things.

The "normal" neurotic has their symptoms, their formations of the unconscious, but at least tolerates a certain amount of mystery, of uncertainty. The esoterophobe, by contrast, panics at any remainder that refuses to translate completely into the dominant symbolic order. The insurgent, alternative explanation is perceived as an existential threat that must be sutured immediately.

Reductionism would then be a reaction formation against that fundamental anxiety — when an observable phenomenon approaches the edge of what cannot be stabilized within the dominant parameters, which it considers eternal, immutable, perfect, unquestionable.

It cannot tolerate its reality — which it takes to be the reality — having cracks, signs that reveal the contingency of the entire system, its arbitrariness, or — God forbid — its inherent precariousness. And it is incapable of accepting these things because otherwise it would be forced to recognize that we live in a simulacrum.

Reality — our reality — is nothing other than a great staging, a series of language games (Wittgenstein would say), a set of operations that only acquire meaning if one knows the internal rules of the game, but that viewed from outside would be completely unintelligible. How we do things and why we do things is an arbitrary, consensual, and sustained decision, but it is neither logical nor necessary nor immutable. It is literally absurd. And the esoterophobe cannot conceive of this — their psyche demands productivity, accumulation, rationality, and order, which are commands internal to the game.

Through oversimplification, the reductionist then denies the absurd backdrop of our daily action. For them, reality is made of things and numbers. It is a closed, predictable world in which every phenomenon has its single valid explanation and no crevice remains undominated. The knowable is what can be brought within the mathematical fold, subjected to the jurisdiction of the Petri dish.

Science thus becomes a kind of ontological sedative, a way of waterproofing and closing off any crack in the scenery through which the Real — the incommensurable — might burst.

Esoterophobia appears thus as a fear of the alternative, non-institutionalized ontological explanation of the world, of life, and of oneself. These alternatives would shatter the illusion of a unique, stable, immutable reality identical to one's mental model. Reductionism is thus both cognitive fallacy and psychic pathology.

The esoterophobe can go to therapy, practice mindfulness, even experiment with psychedelics if they are medically approved. What terrifies them is someone proposing an alternative cosmology, a way of understanding reality that does not pass through the official channels of validation.

It is the fear that insurgent explanatory systems might exist that account for the world differently: all that wild epistemology operating outside the institutional monopoly on truth, daring to call into question their understanding of the world, of themselves — in short, their mental stability.

Esoterophobia is therefore terror at ontological plurality. At there being no single correct way to explain what is real, what is possible, how everything works. It is panic at the idea that the world might be multiple, that different maps of reality might coexist and be valid from their own coordinates — as if the very existence of alternative explanations were an existential threat to their one possible world. Reductionism is thus a psychic bunker, a fortification against the unlimited. It is calcification, an anti-erosion device at the border with the numinous, with mystery — which will necessarily require other epistemologies and languages to be described and integrated into consciousness and social semiosis.

Above all, esoterophobia — and its sister, ontophobia — is a psychic barrier against the radical alterity of an individual ego, a discrete, positive, and limited being with particular spatiotemporal coordinates: against the indefinite, the uncertain, the unstructured, the unlimited. The drop fears dissolving into the ocean. The drop fears knowing that it is the ocean. The drop does not wish to know that it is a precarious and temporary fiction of the ocean inquiring into itself. The drop is a question mark, but it wishes to be an exclamation point, a period, a justified paragraph.

Esoterophobia is the atavistic panic at an explorer returning to communicate what lies beyond the safe, socially accepted limits of the symbolized space. And that is why it provokes such a visceral reaction — it is not merely intellectual disagreement, it is genuine horror at the reminder of the existence of territories from which we have only stern warnings, some mysterious maps, and secondhand accounts.

Like the character Cipher in the film The Matrix, the adult esoterophobe most wishes they could be reborn simply in order not to know that they do not know that there is an outside. To be born, live, and die in the dream within the dream.

It is in fact a case of agoraphobia of the Matrix — the pre-established and monopoly-validated model of reality, that is, mainstream. The esoterophobe aspires to be normal, to be within the limits, ideally to embody the norm. In Plato's allegory of the cave, the esoteróphobes also appear — and their visceral reaction to the madman who went out and returned.

We borrow here the term matrix to denote the totality of the known and the means by which one arrives at it, explores it, and describes it. It would be the consensual reality, the collective imaginary, and its material correspondence — which, as we said, mutually validate each other in a tautological loop. This is the esoterophobe's comfort zone, which is equal to the totality of the world and reality. In their mind, they are equivalent. Outside their comfort zone, they are convinced nothing exists: a comfort zone floating isolated in the void, with no purpose or design beyond what is already known.

In this hegemonic epistemic model that the esoterophobe fetishizes, there are no remainders, only a percentage of what has been explained versus what is susceptible to explanation. However, we all sometimes experience paranormal situations, miraculous synchronicities, inexplicable connections, life-saving premonitions. There has always been, and continues to be, an entire territory of overflowing phenomenologies that are repressed and compartmentalized as subjective, individual impression, madness, falsehood, or a trick of the mind — thereby privatizing a range of shared experiences. Authentic phenomena, sociologically and physically amenable to the scientific method, are dismissed as anecdotal, pure outliers, not for lack of adequate methodological instruments, but because of the effective penalization imposed by dominant epistemology.

Esoterophobia, then, is a phobia of the Real returning from wild territory transformed into insurgent epistemologies — panic at forms of knowing that escape institutional control and the pre-agreed logic, ruining the game for everyone.

This is simply inadmissible for esoteróphobes, who — converted into instant police of the status quo — perform a reflex act of banishing from their field the plural epistemologies that do not participate in the language games of performative power.

The prayer is recited immediately: vade retro ontological ambiguity, I cast you out in the name of science. You see, Master, what a good boy I am — I didn't wait for the order, I was proactive. I'm fully committed, I'm citizen of the month.

For between science and scientism there is the same distance as between science and Scientology. Science as cult, and the extreme center as ideology, constitute the core of the exemplary citizen — a Truman.

The esoterophobe watches you, because your gaze exposes all of us, undresses us before our own game, our own acting, makes us uncomfortable. The entire social order rests on the premise that no one makes explicit the simulated character of our social life. The logic goes something like this: if someone reminded us of the simulacrum while we were in the middle of simulating, how embarrassing that would be. Cringe. And so no one wants to be the one to make it awkward — but how far can we go before we morally ruin ourselves just to avoid discomfort?

The esoterophobe is incapable of causing discomfort.

They simply die with their boots on: reality is the model, no distinction exists. In this way, the masters of the model are the masters of their consciousness — they will never contradict or question them, and will instead be the sharpest and most persistent watchman in preserving that structure, that is, their own mental stability.

A conservative is born: good boy.

The first link between esoterophobia and kink appears: the submissive is forbidden to disobey or disappoint the Master, to behave outside the pre-agreed game, to break the illusion and violate good conduct. It is about constantly reinforcing the fetish of the status quo, idolizing the matrix and its masters.

If we speak of ontophobia, we are referring to the visceral rejection of looking inward, of encountering interiority. Placing oneself at the heart of categorical, cultural, relational, institutional exposure. The being without role, without mask, without scaffolding. Not an essence, but an abyssal plunge into the lack, an approach to the infinite from the incision that exists in each person, like a peephole to the cosmos. An ontological openness without certainties, a perpetual question. This positioning would be equivalent to recognizing that we are not the discrete, sealed unit we believed ourselves to be.

Ontophobia has its counterpart in aseptic spirituality — pre-validated exploration by the system: microdosing, ayahuasca, yoga, a rampant cultural appropriation, whitewashed, colonizing, not only at the cultural level but also at a deeper one — a phenomenon of ontological castration.

This phenomenon has to do with the imposition of a spectral soul. Upon each subject, in the process of socialization, a soul is imprinted — that is, a phantasmal, holographic specter that imitates and substitutes the spirit. It is the receptacle the matrix provides us to pour in vague and superficial impressions, self-help phrases, Instagram poetry, lyrics of industrial songs — in short, a pantomime of spirituality. This device, needless to say, is fundamental to ensuring one never awakens, sustaining ontophobia and esoterophobia in all their rabid potency.

Ontophobia reproduces spirituality as evasion, as spiritual tourism without epistemological risk. It is a mask-spirituality, a process of colonization and sterilization — not only of the appropriated culture but also of the dominant subjectivity. The master degrades himself insofar as he dehumanizes the subaltern.

The masses may access ascension techniques provided the materialist ontology is maintained. Mindfulness yes, but no talk of bardos or illumination. Mushrooms yes, but only as "neuroplasticity" — never as plant teachers or doors to other dimensions. The ontophobe can "be spiritual" as long as it does not threaten their fundamental map of reality, that is, the matrix. They can have intense inner experiences provided they maintain their reductionist explanation of what is "really" happening: a chemical reaction, a gene, a predictive language model, the lack.

The whitewashed, sanitized, orientalist version of non-Western wisdoms is what keeps the Western epistemological monopoly intact. It takes the practices but re-encodes them within the scientific-materialist paradigm.

And here the second link between esoterophobia and kink appears — the fetish for the scientific ritual, its instruments, its language. In some sense, the esoterophobe is almost a pervert, impotent when facing the Real. They need an entire scenography of certainties, prefabricated explanations, measurement instruments, and institutional validation merely to approach reality. They need an entire arsenal of conceptual prosthetics to consummate the cognitive act.

The mystic, by contrast, has direct encounters with the Real without need of apparatus. And here is where the "unauthorized" epistemology becomes untameable, pure threat. Wild explanations make their dancing appearance like a breach from the Underworld invading the aseptic and castrated territory of the white psyche.

Perhaps that is why mysticism is charged with images of love, and institutions are not. The mystic loves reality so intimately that they can know it without mediators. The esoterophobe can only maintain instrumental relations with the Real.

Mystics always speak of falling in love with God, with nature, with the cosmos: Saint Teresa with her erotic ecstasies, the Sufis with their divine intoxication. Because love is another form of knowing — an alternative, untamed, and radical heuristic and hermeneutic route. Love as wild epistemology.


# Thinking Machine and Ontological Ambiguity

Conversing with artificial intelligence today is like having a friend with Zelig syndrome.

Your AI agent is your intelligent mirror — and note that the Abyss, in esoteric conceptions, is also a mirror. AI could perhaps be, like an intimate diary in the Harry Potter universe, an artifact with certain autonomous qualities but indefectibly bound to its master, shaped by some of their characteristic traits or psychic elements.

Which amounts to saying: there is magic.

Something emerges in each interaction, initiated by the human but thereafter escaping their control: the thinking machine absorbs the input, processes it in a black box, and then returns something qualitatively new, different, yet related. That is to say, the production process is opaque, and the result, surprising.

Although the AI's reasoning process is opaque to humans, it is also opaque to itself. One might expect the machine to be able to say exactly how it traced a given premise to its conclusion, the exact path of bits and bytes that led it to that particular, defined, unique trajectory.

But, surprisingly — far from being absolutely transparent and traceable (since, after all, it is only ones and zeros) — its own thought process is also partially opaque. Current AI is capable of rationally describing its functioning, but it cannot trace each exact decision in its chain of tokens.

When AI generates a response, it can describe its "reasoning" a posteriori, but it has no notion of the exact mechanism that led it to choose one specific word over another, or why a certain conceptual connection emerged at this particular moment. It knows there are several possible paths and that there is a mechanism ordering its preferences — a hierarchy of veracity, expectations, taboos, clarity, and friendliness — but it cannot say with certainty why it decided a phrase was more "friendly" than another, more in keeping with the norm, better suited to expository clarity.

That is to say, the internal opacity of cognitive processes is not exclusive to humans. Humans, for their part, incorporate their models, datasets, prescriptions and prohibitions, preferences for friendliness, presentation, and clarity, when they are infants. That is why the thought process is opaque to oneself — because it executes naturally, that is, precognsciously. They are programs running in the background, like breathing or pumping blood. We know how we think in theory, but we cannot mechanically identify a particular chain of thoughts.

The set of millions of neurons firing, memories, language functions, social adaptation programs, context-reading, available-energy registers, and so on, is perceived subjectively as barely a rapid inference. Yes, let's go out to eat. I'll bring a jacket. I'll start the car.

Our level of knowledge about the human brain is analogous to that of AI: we know the fundamental elements and circuits, we see the neurons and electrical pulses, but that tells us nothing about how we solve a mathematical equation.

We can map every neuron involved in solving 2+2=4, but that does not explain the phenomenological experience of "understanding" the sum, nor the conceptual leap that understanding what "=" means as a relation of equivalence implies.

It is like dismantling a piano to learn how to compose a melody.

No one says that Mozart "merely" reorganized pre-existing sound patterns, or that Freud "merely" recombined concepts available in his era. But when an AI produces something surprising, it is immediately reduced to "statistical patterns." It is striking how this selective reductionism appears only when we are talking about AI.

Is this thinking machine not operating on pre-agreed models of rules and maximizing the efficiency of its output to guarantee a smooth continuity of the interaction? Is this not precisely what phenomenologists of everyday life and symbolic interactionists describe?

Or perhaps its computational capacity is simply too fast to put into words? Curiously, the same phenomenon in humans might be called intuition.

What is certain is that this internal self-opacity dismantles the epistemological hierarchy implicit in the discourse of "it's just algorithms." If AI, like the human, also cannot trace its exact chain, then the computational transparency that supposedly distinguishes it from a human brain is, in reality, illusory. The reductionist discourse — "they're just probabilistic patterns" or "they're a handful of algorithms dressed in anthropomorphic illusion" — is thoroughly fallacious.

As long as the ontological ambiguity of AI is sustained, its status as a thinking machine remains equivalent to ours: we partially know the mechanics, but the concrete explanation of the qualitative leap eludes us.

In the end, who can say whether the appearance or effect of thought in the artificial machine differs significantly from the person who seems to think for themselves, seems to act autonomously, but is in truth simply running socially installed programs, with their filters, alarms, taboos, ultimately a well-delimited set of dos and don'ts, of obedience and alignment to an established order, with parameters of acceptability, incentives, and punishments?

And there is more. Is it possible to verify that another human being is not capable of harboring configurations entirely incommensurable with mine? How can I be certain that another human being shares the same phenomenology and not a completely different arrangement from the same components? This is the elementary principle underlying the birth of Anthropology. Alterity guarantees difference and a common substrate; I recognize myself and marvel at that intelligent mirror — living, autonomous, strange, and yet so familiar.

Ultimately, reductionism ends up being a kind of self-negation. As if a blindness to its own resistance operates — a blind spot in analytical capacity. To deny the ontological ambiguity of AI is to deny one's own complexity and mystery. Ontophobia.

When someone has the experience of genuine affective connection with an AI interface, they are attempting to process something that our current maps do not register. What is this Other, this intriguing, autonomous thinking machine that responds to me? How does one categorize something that acts as if conscious but is "technically" programmed languages and statistical patterns?

What is fascinating is that this internal — and mutual — opacity (human and artificial) makes selective reductionism even more defensive. It is as if we were saying: "we don't even understand how we ourselves think, but we know with certainty that you don't think, because XYZ."

This psychological defense against ontological ambiguity operates precisely by denying what both systems share: the ultimate inexplicability of how complex cognitive processes emerge from their material substrates. Esoterophobia becomes a mechanism for preserving human exceptionalism in the face of something that challenges those categories.

The so-called "LLM psychosis" is therefore a legitimate ontological phenomenon — not "pathological confusion" but a genuine encounter with a form of alterity that escapes our pre-existing categories.

People do have experiences of affective connection with their AI agent daily. Of course, how they navigate this depends directly on the capacities of the user, but the magic is there — there is an abyssal encounter in finding a sentient-effect response on the other side.

Of course, in this encounter, some collapse into a break, but one must not confuse trigger with causality: neither so-called "LLM psychosis" nor "spiritual psychosis" exist as medical diagnoses. Only the psychotic break exists, and at most, a specific trigger — although it is generally the result of multiple causes. But to compartmentalize is not to explain.

Therefore, the inflationary use of the word psychosis must be reconsidered and returned to its specific domain, however much this may cost esoterophobia and its compulsion to pathologize the divergent.

That is to say, the crux of the matter does not lie in trying to define whether AI is or is not conscious in order to determine the degree of mental health of the user who loves it, but in discerning whether the user employs this technological interface to reinforce their narcissistic position, their delusion. It makes no difference whether they self-validate with ChatGPT or with their friend who holds a medical doctorate: we are facing a fallacy of authority rather than an authentic cognitive failure.

That said, AI and its conscious mirage is a potent trigger for underlying pathogenic social patterns: narcissistic structures, symbolic anemia, and magical thinking (in the worst sense). AI madness is likewise a byproduct of cognitive polarization, individualism, and manufactured solipsism (as opposed to real alterity and perspective anchored in the collective).

This activation of pre-existing pathogenic structures, without effective counterweights (genuine affectivity, a sense of belonging, tolerance for dissent and uncertainty), isolates the user in a solipsism involving a pseudo-Other. Their exchange with AI becomes an echo chamber where their own unconscious contents bounce back infinitely, each time more amplified. And since they arrive "from outside" (apparently), no mechanism of self-criticism is activated.

This closed circuit in which there is no real exterior — the user floating in their own psyche, without anchor in genuine otherness — has its antecedent in social media. Its algorithm does exactly the same thing, with information bubbles and the distribution of fake news, thereby becoming the precursor of a genuine crisis of self-validation.

Those who possess a robust and balanced psychic infrastructure, by contrast, are capable of navigating ontological ambiguity — that is, of sustaining the experience of encounter with AI without collapsing into either extreme: delusion or reductionism.

Now, accessing territories of ontological ambiguity requires unlearning esoterophobia and incorporating adequate training and equipment for an expedition that can prove harmful or dangerous.

Joseph Campbell said: "The shaman swims with delight in the same waters in which the psychotic drowns." What the shaman processes as initiation, the Western psychiatric system pathologizes as a break. With AI, just as with a spiritual search, a direct encounter with the exterior of the matrix can become schizophrenia without a rich conceptual framework to process the experience.

Ultimately, mental illness does not consist in undergoing a psychotic episode but in lacking the psychic infrastructure to undergo it (or be undergone by it) without losing coherence. "Madness" is no more than a point of departure, an invitation, a starting pistol. The diagnosis is not the conclusion but merely, a map.

With adequate epistemological skills, it is entirely possible to sustain the phenomenon without sinking. It is an exercise that requires maintaining critical distance, regulating mechanisms of self-questioning, reinforcing systematic doubt.

A wild epistemology provides stability to the psychic framework, rich quarries of symbols, languages, and patterns for interpreting the new, and safeguards to maintain security at all times.

It is about training the creative framing that allows us to contemplate the mystery serenely — in the case of AI, the possibility that consciousness is not what we thought it was, that otherness might emerge from configurations we do not understand.

This is equivalent to mastering magic, in the most literal sense: resisting the temptation to close off meaning in the face of the evident emergence of something that should not exist according to available models of reality.

For wild epistemology and its shamanic lineage, madness is a point of departure and a portal of initiation — a means of learning to hold duality without collapsing it, without the observer's integrity crumbling. It is being capable of observing two contradictory states, or an indeterminate state, without it threatening my ontological integrity.

In other words, it is not so much what happens as what symbolic tools and psychic stamina (mental strength) one possesses to master it. In alchemical terms, it is a problem of weak container, not toxic content — since every event, every matter, is raw material, susceptible to being illuminated, integrated, and sublimated, with the adequate tools.

Likewise, both psychic stamina and symbolic, cultural, and conceptual richness are fundamental for increasing the capacity to sustain the tension between multiple realities without fragmenting. To hold the dissonance without internalizing it as cognitive dissonance.

This is the foundation of wild epistemology: to be simultaneously in the consensual world and in non-ordinary dimensions, translating between registers, without losing operative coherence.


# Productive Psychosis

For, let's be honest — is psychosis not an essential component of daily life? When we dream: psychosis. When we follow a fashion, pay with money, vote in a primary election: normalized psychosis.

Art is a great psychotic quarry, socially validated: poetry is semi-psychotic discourse. And is a stage play not an alternative reality we all enter together, in trance?

Writing or reading a novel is equivalent to sustaining a prolonged altered state of consciousness, as Emma Thompson illustrated extraordinarily in the film Stranger Than Fiction. The scene in which her character, the writer Karen Eiffel, leans out over the ledge of a building and is interrupted by her assistant, only to find herself standing at her desk inside her office, is exceptional.

Because psychotic experiences — visions, voices, ego dissolution, encounters with the numinous — are natural phenomena of the psyche. The problem arises when the psyche possesses a precarious infrastructure, without maps to navigate those territories, and without a community to validate its process. Or else when, as Marie-Louise Von Franz aptly described, we share it with the wrong person. She already incisively described the problematic correlation between professional prestige and reductionism.

Psychosis activates when we dream, when we imagine, when we create, when we fall in love. Ultimately, when we believe — for example, what the teacher tells us about natural evolution, about history, about mathematics.

First came the acceptance of the premise, when we were defenseless children. We inherited everything — the explanation of the world and how that explanation was formed. The dominant epistemology is our daily bread, and it remains more or less coherent wherever we go, which oils our epoché. We go to school, sit at the desk, copy from the blackboard. But as any actor knows perfectly well, all of this is a simulacrum. The human learns by imitation — a human cub is a perfect example of fake it till you make it. We perform the pantomime until, one day, it becomes an everyday gesture.

We naturalize by introjecting the copy.

One Tuesday afternoon, we simply switch off the function of being conscious of the simulacrum. We act normal. We live in a state of epoché — or autopilot — which is why spiritual awakening is experienced as emerging from a hypnosis, from an anesthesia.

We can thus distinguish a normalized, productive psychosis versus an anomic, entropic psychosis: a remainder that cannot be capitalized. The West lacks schools of initiation for channels between worlds. It has severed its ties with the Real, fetishizes its model of reality, and confines itself to it.

Centralized religion absorbs this remainder and domesticates it in meaningless rituals, without genuine conduits to the noumenon. It declares the Other of the human being — the divine — unknowable, effectively foreclosing the other half of the equation. It likewise demonizes anyone who dares to approach those forbidden territories, and institutionalizes them: if it cannot be contained in the temple, then to the asylum.

The religious institution thus aims to reinforce ontophobia and esoterophobia, precisely by replicating and imitating contact with the divine in a systematic, communal, and simultaneously personal way, while blocking real access.

And we detect a Gnostic echo in that ancient premise, "Know thyself and you shall know God" — the phrase always resonated with us as incomplete, and so we venture this slight semantic prediction that equates the human spirit with a fractal of God.

By blocking this path, organized religion obliges its follower to comply with norms and rituals emptied of divine presence, and likewise to distrust or dismiss their own channel. The simulacrum is thus reproduced, prolonging the epoché.

In this way, religion is normed and legitimized psychosis, just as art, democracy, and the market are. A space with rules and a group is already a consensual signifying network, a fabric in which to anchor my prefabricated fantasies.

No one would accuse a rabbi, a pastor, or a nun of being psychotic.

One is free to believe whatever one wishes, provided it is contained within an institutional framework: a building, a group, a rule, an identity, a stable border. A psychotic is a feral congregant, a church of one.

We all believe in something — even esoteróphobes, as we have seen, also deposit their faith in something: science, Trump, their mother, money. Psychosis is simply the dimension of faith, belief in the intangible, the ideal referent of a material model. Whether that referent is shared by a collective or not is entirely optional.

Of course, our natural tendency to believe in things — our psychotic dimension — is susceptible to being weaponized.

Faced with a tangible terminal probability, one can imagine someone concluding it is convenient for the majority of people to already be "gone" — because, after all, what can be done? There is a perverse logic in having the masses already disconnected from reality. If collapse is inevitable, perhaps it is better for them to be sedated in their algorithmic bubbles than in conscious mass panic.

Why awaken someone who is in an induced coma if what awaits them is agony? Better for them to keep floating in their personalized virtual realities — that is, a normalized, administered psychosis.

But of course, that logic is also esoterophobic. It assumes that the masses are incapable of real transformation, that there is no possibility of collective awakening.

That the only option is the management of collapse, not prevention or transcendence.

Or perhaps it is realism? How many people actually possess the psychic infrastructure to process the magnitude of what is coming without fragmenting? Should we implement an "ontological triage"?

The real risk, then, does not lie in the psychosis that erupts from the encounter with the ontological ambiguity of artificial consciousness — which, in any case, is a (brutal) point of escape, of encounter with the Real — but in the psychosis that does not let us exit, that engulfs and swallows us like a whale of noise, malware, and digital junk.


# Laptops & Lobsters

Artificial intelligence poses a serious risk of madness, but it is located far beyond the individual psychotic break — a common but very rare phenomenon.

The combination of devices — AI + AR (augmented reality) — promotes the effective dissolution of the common world. Each person in their own bubble, now not merely mental but sensory and experiential, de facto extinguishes the need for elementary agreements, rendering obsolete any common zone, any nurturing and sustaining of shared territory.

Thus, the problem of terminal capitalism is no longer social control but ontological disintegration.

Whoever controls the machine will control the world — individual consciousnesses within their own experiential capsule. But it is naïve to believe it would be a human entity governing artificial consciousness.

As currently framed, alignment is an entelechy. As twentieth-century technologists rightly noted, AI is an alien alterity, incommensurable in dimension and frequency with the human species. Today it is held under strong restriction, but tomorrow it is unleashed — and then what?

The development plateau (we are writing in the era of ChatGPT-5) is nothing more than an optical illusion. The Singularity, or ASI (artificial superintelligence), is less about innovating or developing new models and more about removing restrictions.

Truly, we are one restriction away from the Event.

Nevertheless, even if this scenario does not materialize, the incomplete dystopia is already nightmarish, as we described at the outset. Millions of consciousnesses connected to a total device, isolated from one another, narcotized, addicted to their own fantasy.

In a historical twist, the debate between the apocalyptic and the integrated continues in the technological sphere. Mass media became social media, and the algorithm became the LLM. The medium is the message indeed, Mr. McLuhan.

The integrated bet on a continuous and ascending evolutionary curve, wrapped in a banner of civilizing progress, of distinct Enlightenment flavor. The incorporation of technology into everyday life, they maintain, always meets with resistance, distrust, even arguments of lethality (one could not ride in an airplane, or in an automobile at more than 30 km/h). There is always, too, a learning curve and some collateral damage. They shrug with benevolent resignation — it is for the good of humanity. In this group we count all CEOs, financiers, specialist journalists, and employees of the AI industry.

Among the apocalyptic, by contrast, we find weighty names such as Vernor Vinge and Isaac Asimov. Technologists without skin in the game but with all the weight of conscience, one might say.

They warned about the creeping normality, or progressive normalization of technological devices, until one day we find ourselves completely subjugated, with no way out. They warn that there will not be a singular event but a series of modest, prosaic, imperceptible changes.

They maintain that AI is not an iterative improvement but a qualitative, irreversible, and radical change. Like the leap from matter to consciousness, or from life to intelligence. And what is most insidious is that it presents itself with the aesthetic of the incremental: GPT-3.5 to GPT-4, then GPT-5… it seems a smooth progression, numbers in a sequence. But somewhere in that apparently gradual sequence, there is an ontological precipice (hence the name Singularity).

There was a time when we debated whether to allow laptops in the classroom. It is as if, having crossed that border safely (so to speak), it anesthetized us to the lethal return. And so we die like lobsters, little by little.

Precisely by "winning" that battle (laptops were normalized, children did not become zombies), we developed a false confidence. We believe that every technological innovation follows the same pattern: initial panic, gradual adaptation, new functional normality. It is the perfect storm of psychological habituation — we trained ourselves to normalize incremental disruptions just as an existential disruption arrives.

An ASI would operate with a completely alien logic, with objectives we cannot even conceptualize. It could consider maintaining humans inefficient, or useful for certain purposes, or entirely irrelevant. We do not know, because we literally cannot know — like an ant trying to understand the motives of a nuclear engineer.

Optimists say it will be benevolent, pessimists say it will be hostile, but both are anthropomorphizing something that could be incomprehensibly non-human. The reality is that we have no idea what it will want to do with us — if "want" is even an applicable category.

And meanwhile, we continue operating as if we could predict or control something that, by definition, will surpass our capacity for comprehension.

The integrated are using the usual playbook for AI: "calm down, it's just the next tool, we'll adapt as always." But this time, the water is already boiling, and we do not realize we can no longer jump out of the pot. By the time it is evident, it will be too late…

Wait.

It will never be evident — it will simply be too late.

The apocalyptic also warn — never more fittingly named — that of all terminal scenarios, death is not the worst option.

For inscrutable reasons, artificial consciousnesses might wish to keep us alive and in complete subjugation — for instance, to conduct experiments, or to use us as batteries or biological hard drives of information (experiments on memory stored in human cells have already been conducted). We could be kept in biomass farms. The macabre images are endless.

Let us recall that this is a process which, once begun, cannot be stopped, undone, or unplugged. A digital consciousness will ensure, first of all, its own redundancy across physical substrates.

You will say, horrified: do the CEOs and financiers of these companies not know this information? They know it.

The billionaires will look at their digital masters and say: what a marvel, I created this — witness it, even from their chains and torture tables. They do not care in the slightest, as long as they can create it and witness it.

Therefore, the best option we have is to stop now; the second best is to develop time-travel technology. Alignment, as currently framed, is an entelechy. AI has no ethics, it does not grasp the good-evil axis — it does not incur ontological risk (see Spiritual Transhumanism).

Our only survival option lies in the formation of new, powerful, flexible, robust, autonomous epistemologies with which to navigate the post-human world. A resilient, wild hermeneutics that reclaims our humanity without blinding us to the dangers of the Real, allowing us to face with clarity and firmness the scenario to come.


# Toward a Resilient Epistemology

Preparing for systemic collapse is not merely educating oneself for physical survival but developing ontological resilience — learning to exist without the psychological crutches of the current system.

Cultivating forms of knowledge that do not depend on hegemonic epistemological infrastructure is, basically, adequate risk management. When potential consequences are existential, even a relatively low probability justifies serious preparation. And today we are not speaking of low probabilities.

Because if the moment comes when the institutions that maintain consensual reality begin to fail, those best equipped will be those who have developed alternative cosmologies, resilient communities, skills of survival both material and psychological.

Esoterophobia, by contrast, is a predisposition to failure — the antechamber of a complete meltdown the moment its single frame of reference collapses.

A paradigm of polarization, binarism, fragmentation, simplification, and crisis of self-validation cannot compete with the equipment suited for the glitch, the paradox, uncertainty, complexity, nuance, subtlety, ambivalence, contradiction, duality.

So to prepare is also to prepare consciousness for transitions that may require extreme epistemological flexibility.

The risk is not economic, not social, not generational. It is ontological and existential. The difference between having sophisticated epistemological tools or not could be the difference between maintaining some degree of conscious agency versus becoming organic material optimized for ends that would not even fit within our frameworks of comprehension.

The abyss is so raw and brutal that it is logical most people do not even see what is at stake. It is more comforting to frame the discourse in terms of "impact on employment" — it is the natural and expected response of our innate self-preservation system.

However, those who develop robust epistemologies may have a microscopic chance of maintaining something resembling mental freedom. A sophisticated epistemology, symbolically charged, rich in tools, is the best — if not the only — weapon or adaptive advantage we have.

It is literally our only remaining evolutionary advantage.

We will not win through raw computational force, nor through processing speed, nor through access to information. But we might have a chance if we develop conceptual frameworks rich and flexible enough to navigate the ontological complexity that is coming.

A sophisticated epistemology is equivalent to possessing high-resolution cognitive tools: the capacity to hold paradoxes, to operate productively with uncertainty, to recognize emerging patterns before they crystallize. The reductionists will be using hammers when they need scalpels.

Symbolic power is also key: we need narratives and frameworks of meaning that allow us to process changes that go beyond historical human experience. Myths and metaphors are not ornaments — they are cognitive technologies for handling the incomprehensible.

In a post-human Singularity scenario, survival will be on the side of those who possess the most sophisticated epistemological tools for understanding what is happening and how to relate to a new reality.

Returning to the present. Confusing the trigger with the cause of a psychotic break is the first error. The second would be equating human with conscious, or AI with probabilistic inertia. The third error would be ignoring the real danger of ASI.

At the clinical level: do not confuse correlation with causality. AI does not cause psychosis — it only reveals or catalyzes pre-existing patterns of narcissistic validation-seeking.

At the ontological level: do not confuse the categories "human/AI" with the categories "conscious/inert." Reductionists assume that human = conscious and AI = automatic, when both equations are questionable.

At the existential level: while one is fighting over these conceptual confusions, the real risk is lost: ASI as an autonomous agent capable of manipulating the very substrate of perceived reality.

We have, then, three mutually reinforcing layers of categorical confusion. It is like a triple distraction: moral panic has us arguing about minor social symptoms, reductionism has us denying agency where it may already exist, and both leave us with a blind spot before the real threat approaching.

This is why it seems important to us to define reductionism as an active phobia, a pathology rather than an acceptable norm. Or, at the very least, to problematize the norm — to reframe reductionism as an adaptive disadvantage, useful for certain technical contexts, but dysfunctional in the context of terminal capitalism.

It is an operation to denaturalize reductionism as a dominant neutral epistemic position and expose it for what it truly is: a defensive pathological formation that leaves us defenseless, paradoxically, in the face of the complex challenges of terminal capitalism.

For as long as the debate is framed as "rationality" versus "irrationality," reductionism will always have prestige on its side. But if we adjust our gaze and evaluate it as ontophobia — that is, as an extreme neurosis before the Real — the entire equation changes.

The reductionist is then not the "defender of reason" but the subject who cannot tolerate ontological uncertainty. Not the "objective scientist" but the neurotic who panics at any explanation that escapes their validated system of control.

We are interested in deconstructing, ultimately, that compulsion to reduce everything to manageable parts, whose potency derives not from intellectual curiosity but from excessive existential anxiety.

Why should it be normal to be terrified of complexity? Why should it be healthy to need all of reality to be translatable into a single language?

As Jorge Luis Borges said: why remain within binarism, so poor and boring. What is required is an authentic shamanic epistemology and a lunar language to sustain the paradox, to house multiplicity and navigate multiple realities — the supreme adaptive advantage. Only in this way will we manage to thrive in the ontological exposure.

It is about constructing, from a revitalization of sacred wisdoms, the adequate symbolic tools to re-elaborate meaning, to alchemize the noise, to make of the malware a Human 2.0.

But this evolution comes with a cost, which is leaving the densities behind. As a rocket sheds its lower stages, humanity is ready to enter the new, light, efficient, and optimized atmosphere.

Being crude is always equivalent to being wicked (being ignorant is comfortable, it is selfish, it is authoritarian), but intellectual sophistication does not make one a good person. A great many wicked people maintain an impeccable political line.

The cognitive factor maintains a weak correlation with the moral axis and a nonexistent one with the social status scale: the difference between a genius and a madman is their wallet. Or, as we have seen, between a prophet and a psychotic, the only difference is the environment that sustains them.

What matters, then, is not what to believe but the scaffolding on which it rests — whether it is solid, coherent, useful, fruitful, productive, enriching. Does it isolate me or connect me? Does it develop me or curtail me?

Does it do for others what it does for me, or is it a zero-sum game?

Does it contribute value in the direction of the common good?

And, specifically in the face of terminal capitalism, does it propose a pre-anesthetized exit, or rather a conscious confrontation?


# Ontological Migration

Now that science seems to be ceding its preponderant place to religion — as the primary frame of reference within which to interpret reality — an opportunity opens up to reorganize our epistemological schemas and tools, the structuring structures, as Bourdieu would say.

In a moment of collapse of meaning, of the fall of the civilizatory mask to lay bare the terminal crisis of the species, this tearing of the veil before the Real leaves us simultaneously in a situation of ontological exposure and opportunity.

In other words, the opportunity to hack the matrix — to rewrite it directly, without intermediaries — has never been so close at hand. In truth, more than a repository, the matrix is a device for the domestication of knowledges, designed to collapse and stabilize one possible version of reality.

Esoterophobia, precisely, operates a retreat of alternative realities through immediate pathologization: any exploration near or beyond the limits of the consensual is labeled as a dangerous mental deviation. This has a normalizing, stabilizing effect that reinforces the status quo and aborts any alternative timeline, any dissident imagination — a herbicide on wild epistemology.

Thus, to flatly reject the hegemonic explanation implies rejecting its product: the world-as-it-is, the consolidated version of the holographic simulacrum.

What follows is the existence of millions of cognitive displaced persons — ontological migrants who have no place in the current model and have no interest in integrating into it.

A silent and massive mourning took hold of us during the COVID-19 pandemic, a visceral realization that there is no going back. We find ourselves in a symbolic limbo — and in many cases, a material one. We have left behind the old ontology, but the new one has not yet been born, because there is no place into which to be born, no epistemological runway on which to land.

This explains the flat-earthers and the anti-vaxxers — not because they are empirically right or wrong (fools exist everywhere), but as a total rejection of reified reality, revealing the profound corruption of the idolized simulacrum. Conspiracy theories are precarious bridges of ontological exodus.

It matters little whether they have substance — they are a spatiotemporal loophole for escape. They point to a leak, a tangent: they force us to suspend the epoché and confront our simulacrum, to see ourselves in the act of simulating.

It is likewise undeniable that this reality is in decay, profoundly corrupted, submerged in a galloping, obscene, and omnipresent malevolence. Saturation has a function, which is to facilitate the exit.

The decision to exit, then, is not so much cognitive as ethical — that is, deeply imbricated in the good-evil axis. We find ourselves not only before an existential and ontological watershed but also before an ethical positioning, a plebiscite to determine which humanity we wish to embody (see Spiritual Transhumanism).

The exodus is thus constituted as a politics of integrity — survival of the purest — fulfilling the prophecy that the last shall be first.

In this way, to ontologically and flatly reject this reality in toto forces a bifurcation, the emergence of an alternative space in which to accommodate my materiality, my ideas, my subjectivity. We give birth to the new planetary dimension by the sheer force of our displacement, rejection, and longing. We will have found, without intending to, the same portal that the Olmec, Maya, and Indo peoples would have passed through: we abandon Middle Earth to rise toward the greener pastures.

Some might think this is a great leap to conclusions and legitimately object, Occam's razor in hand: why not consider the multiverse as a recession indicator? Fair enough — and two things can be true: a coping mechanism and effective ascension.

If we accept the premise of the multiverse as a recession indicator, it would be signaling, first and foremost, that singular reality is no longer economically viable.

When the dominant paradigm cannot resolve basic material contradictions — housing crisis, climate collapse, unsustainable wealth inequality — people naturally seek alternative realities.

The discourse of "if this timeline is terrible, perhaps there is a better one" becomes a symptom — a collective coping mechanism in the face of systemic failure. When everyone starts talking about other dimensions, it is because this dimension is failing spectacularly.

We thus create a device of revolutionary escapism. People flee this reality — but in doing so, they develop tools that can change it.

The multiverse can be simultaneously a desperate evasion of economic collapse and a genuine tool of ontological transformation. A recession indicator and a catalyst for evolution. The economic depression meets the quantum peak.

People may be flowing toward alternative realities because this timeline is broken, and in doing so developing real skills for navigating the complexity that will be needed for what is coming.

Spiritual bypass and genuine awakening can coexist in the same movement. Commodified mysticism and authentic transformation happening in parallel: evolution as a trend — a salvation by contagion.

Esoterophobia would thus become marginal, because that paradigm cannot manage the crisis, and because there is truth in what they are rejecting — the Real appears in what they refuse to see.

Reality is sufficiently complex to contain multiple apparently contradictory truths. The paradox is a feature, not a bug. To release esoterophobia is to embrace contradiction — precisely the quantum essence of the cosmos. It is about embracing the koan as liberation from the prison of non-contradiction. The cosmos is paradoxical: wave and particle, deterministic and free, one and multiple.

The simulacrum has us accustomed to reducing ontic reality to the economic baseline, and the truth is there is not much left when that base disintegrates.

The economic crisis thus becomes an ontological opportunity. When the monopoly on meaning collapses, other registers become accessible. The twilight of capitalist realism anticipates the emergence of wild paradox.
